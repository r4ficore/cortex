Cortex landing copy revision — 2026-10-19

Purpose
Replace vague lifestyle copy with clear situations, session benefits, the actual
product mechanism, and concrete research findings in Polish and English.

Changes
- New first-screen proposition: less tension and more room for focus.
- Five expanded paths: need, distinct sound/breathing experience, named session,
  duration, and preparation link. Unwind now opens Equilibrium rather than the
  breathing ritual. Campaign presets still preserve saved user preferences.
- Four explanatory sections: breathing/autonomic regulation, auditory rhythms,
  sound and unwinding, and evening light. Primary sources link beside each claim.
- The former generic disclaimer is replaced with useful controls and saved-setup
  benefits. Existing whitepaper discussion of product evidence, conflicting
  findings, and protocol limits is retained.
- No audio parameters, clinical claims, player controls or stimulation settings
  have been changed. Research sample sizes, durations and comparisons refer to
  those studies, not measurements of Cortex users.

Primary sources checked
1. Tharion et al., 2012, PMID 23029969:
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23029969/
   36 healthy adults, one month of daily six-breaths/min practice versus control;
   changes in selected HRV indices. Does not establish an immediate three-minute
   Cortex effect or a change in sympathovagal balance.
2. Lane et al., 1998, PMID 9423966:
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9423966/
   29 participants, 30-minute vigilance task; 16/24 Hz beats versus 1.5/4 Hz.
   Better detections/fewer false alarms in that comparison, not validation of
   Cortex's individual frequency sequences or guaranteed productivity.
3. Orozco Perez et al., 2020:
   https://www.eneuro.org/content/7/2/ENEURO.0232-19.2020
   Binaural/monaural signal and auditory-pathway distinction. Used to explain
   binaural perception, not to claim whole-brain synchronisation.
4. Mallik & Russo, 2022:
   https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0259312
   163 participants taking anxiolytics, 24-minute music/ABS/combined/noise arms.
   The stated cognitive-anxiety advantage is limited to the moderate trait-
   anxiety subgroup and the tested music-plus-theta intervention.
5. Chang et al., 2015, PMID 25535358:
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4313820/
   12-person crossover study, four hours of evening reading for five evenings.
   Supports the relevance of evening light, not a quantified sleep improvement
   from Cortex's black-screen mode.
