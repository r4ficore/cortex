Cortex — research-linked session explanations, 2026-10-19

Scope
14 audio protocols and all 3 breathing patterns, PL/EN, standalone preparation
and the 3 daily rituals. Existing purpose/practice descriptions remain available.
The visible frequency summary is derived from audio L/R differences, not the
visual stimulation rate. AM and breath cycles are explained separately.

Presentation
Selection rationale and actual frequency sequence are visible before starting.
The optional research disclosure leads with measured findings, gives a comparison
with ordinary music, explains auditory physiology, and links directly to papers.
Start is not gated by reading. Research links open separately from the player.
The breathing disclosure updates when the selected pattern changes.

Evidence selection and limits
Primary human experiments are indexed with URLs and bilingual findings in
assets/js/session-science.js. Positive and null attention results are both shown.
Beta: Beauchene 2016 (working memory, including a classical-music comparison),
Lane 1998 (vigilance, slower beats as comparator), Robison 2022 (null findings).
Alpha: Solca 2016 (auditory cortical coherence; not a relaxation-outcome trial).
Creativity: Reedijk 2013 (10/40 Hz, variable divergent-thinking results; not 6 Hz).
Calm: McConnell 2014 (post-exercise HRV, 5.5–8.5 Hz) and Mallik/Russo 2022
(music plus theta beats; anxiety outcomes depend on subgroup and component).
Sleep: Lee 2024 (ten-person pilot, dynamic 0–3 Hz, staged PSG delivery;
not the fixed 1 Hz Cortex sequence and not a music-controlled trial).
Gamma: Melnichuk 2025 and Leistik 2023 (auditory stimulation and attention;
different carriers, modulation, durations and controls from Cortex).
Mechanism: Orozco Perez 2020 (auditory response is distinct from mood benefit).
Breath: Joseph 2005 (6/min and baroreflex sensitivity), Balban 2023 (Box among
daily practices), Vierra 2022 (4–7–8 physiological observations without a
non-practice resting control).

Interpretation rules
Session selection is a design application, not validation of each preset.
Do not turn EEG band names into guaranteed mental states or infer dopamine,
direct vagus stimulation, REM control, or physical energy from stimulus Hz.
Do not transfer light-stimulation outcomes to binaural audio or the converse.
Do not treat three breathing patterns as sharing the same six-breaths/min data.
Keep direct comparisons with music distinct from comparisons with silence,
control tones and slower beats. No universal superiority over music is claimed.
