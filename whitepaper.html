The essentials

Cortex brings together sound sessions, guided breathing and quiet visuals. Five paths — Breathe, Focus, Unwind, Sleep and Energy — organise the experience around your intention. These names describe the purpose of a session, not a guaranteed biological outcome.

Research on a technique and research on a product

The publications cited here did not study this version of Cortex. We do not present the app as a clinically validated treatment, a neuroprotective intervention or a device that measures brain activity.

What you hear

Sessions use sine tones separated between the left and right channels, plus optional noise. Tone frequencies and levels follow the stages of the selected protocol. You can adjust tones and background volume separately.

A binaural beat is a perceptual phenomenon associated with presenting different tones to the two ears. This is distinct from evidence that the whole brain has shifted to a chosen frequency. Ingendoh and colleagues found inconsistent results in research on this kind of entrainment. [1]

Parameters are not measurements

The numbers in session details describe the generated sound. Cortex does not perform EEG, identify sleep stages or measure focus. Your post-session rating is a subjective record only.

What we know about breathing

Fincham and colleagues’ meta-analysis reported promising findings for breathwork and self-reported stress, while noting limitations in study quality and variation between studies. [2]

A later trial with an active control did not find coherent breathing superior to the comparison breathing practice. This is a reason not to attribute unique, certain effects to one breathing ratio. [3]

In Cortex, 4–6 is a simple guide: 4 seconds in and 6 seconds out. It is not a diagnosis or an individual prescription. Patterns with breath holds are optional. If a rhythm feels uncomfortable, return to natural breathing.

The NHS recommends gentle, regular breathing without forcing its depth. That principle informs our exercise instructions. [4]

Visuals and stimulation

The default animation is a quiet decoration. During breathing practice, its changing size guides inhalation and exhalation. The movement does not represent a brain signal or track your actual breathing.

Advanced pulsation requires a separate choice before a session. It is limited to at most two cycles per second, a small change in the form’s opacity and no full-screen flashing. We do not use red flashes. Reduced motion disables pulsation.

W3C describes limits on flashing as an accessibility requirement. Meeting an interface criterion is not medical certification or a guarantee of comfort for every person. [5]

Limits and considered use

Your experience can depend on preference, surroundings and circumstances. We do not promise a specific time to fall asleep, improved N3 sleep, anxiety treatment, altered brain metabolism or a guaranteed rise in energy. A frequency-band label alone does not establish these outcomes.

Start at a comfortable, low volume. Stop if you feel uncomfortable. Use Cortex somewhere you can safely give the session your attention. See the guide for practical instructions.
